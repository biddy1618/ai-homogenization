Hi Mark,

Thanks, both points were right. Updated draft attached (v0.3).

(1) Methods. I removed the draft note and rewrote the passage so it describes what we actually do:
clean each answer, split it into sentences, pack them into chunks, embed each chunk and average.
The 128 tokens now appear only as the model's reading limit, which is why chunks are capped at 120
tokens. Nothing in the analysis is truncated.

(2) Tables and Exploratory analyses. Clean copies are below. They match the attached PDF.

(3) One substantive change I should flag. While preparing v0.3 I ran a robustness check reviewers
usually ask for: placing the "ChatGPT break" at fake dates before 2022. The test we used finds a
"significant rise" at most of those fake dates too, because the similarity series bend slowly rather
than turn sharply. So the data show that answers are becoming more alike, but they cannot pin the
start of that to ChatGPT's launch: on several sites it began earlier, and only Cross Validated shows
a clear turn right at 2022Q4.

What still holds, and is now better supported:
  - answers are converging in recent years, mostly in expert and academic communities;
  - the rise is larger on high-cognitive-load sites, confirmed with an objective text-complexity
    measure, not only our own labels;
  - the change is in which questions are asked: on demanding sites the questions that remain are
    longer and harder, consistent with simple questions moving to AI tools;
  - people still do not write like the model.

So v0.3 keeps ChatGPT's release as the reference date we chose in advance, but presents it as
"consistent with, not specific to" ChatGPT (new Section 4.2, and the abstract, discussion and
limitations are updated). I'd rather we say this ourselves than have a reviewer find it. Happy to
walk you through it on our next call; the remaining choice of how strong the headline should be is
in the orange box in the Discussion.

Best,
Dauren

Appendix A. Exploratory analyses

Before the main analysis we ran several simpler measures. They are kept in the
code and results (folder artifacts/exploratory) but superseded:

Family | Measure                              | Finding and why superseded
-------|--------------------------------------|---------------------------
1      | Lexical diversity and surface features | Rises after ChatGPT only because answers got longer; flat at fixed length.
2      | Length-window sweep                  | Confirms family 1 at 50–200-token windows.
3      | LSA (bag-of-words) similarity        | Length artifact like family 1; replaced by sentence embeddings.
4      | LDA topics                           | Topic mix nearly unchanged; replaced by embedding and tag topics.
6      | BERTopic topics                      | Leaves about half of the texts unassigned, so within-topic results describe a selected half; replaced by families 13 and 14, kept for topic labels.
7      | High- vs low-score answers           | Both tiers rise (two sites); the rise is not confined to low-quality answers.
